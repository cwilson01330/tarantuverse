# Terrestrial isopod research for Tarantuverse care guides (2026-11-02)

Final species list (none replaced):
1. Cristarmadillidium muricatum
2. Porcellio scaber
3. Porcellio dilatatus ("Giant Canyon")
4. Armadillo officinalis
5. Merulanella sp. "Ember Bee" (kept, but see section: undescribed trade name, genus unsettled)
6. Porcellio hoffmannseggii (note the double "i"; "hoffmannseggi" is the common misspelling)

How to read this file
- Confidence = high / medium / low, followed by the number of independent sources that agree. "Hobby-consensus only" means no scientific source was found; the figure comes from sellers and hobby care sheets.
- Almost every husbandry figure here comes from sellers or hobby sites, not from published studies. They are written by people who also sell the animal. Treat ranges as "what keepers report", not measured tolerances.
- "Search excerpt only" means I saw the figure only in a web-search result summary and did not open the page. Re-check before publishing.
- Values I recommend leaving null are marked NULL.
- Taxonomy sources used throughout: WoRMS Isopoda database (REST API, fetched 2026-11-02), GBIF backbone (API, fetched 2026-11-02), Wikipedia (secondary, for range and behaviour).
- Conversions: mm / 25.4 = inches; °F = °C x 9/5 + 32.

Cross-cutting notes
- Wikimedia Commons, the Wikipedia API and the iNaturalist API returned empty results through the fetch tool, so no file page could be opened. Photo licences could NOT be verified for any species (see Photos section at the end). Do not use any image until the licence is read on the file page.
- Leaf Litter Lab (leaf-litter-lab.pages.dev) looks like a small, low-effort site with no named author; I used it only as a weak data point and flagged it.
- No source found anything about US import or legal restrictions on any of these six species. Value: NULL for all six. This is "not found", not "confirmed none".

## 1. Cristarmadillidium muricatum

**Accepted name and authority:** Cristarmadillidium muricatum (Budde-Lund, 1885). Status accepted.
- WoRMS: https://www.marinespecies.org/aphia.php?p=taxdetails&id=259924 (AphiaID 259924; REST: https://www.marinespecies.org/rest/AphiaRecordsByName/Cristarmadillidium%20muricatum)
- GBIF: gives the same authority and status ACCEPTED, usageKey 2203545 (https://api.gbif.org/v1/species/match?name=Cristarmadillidium%20muricatum&verbose=true)
- Confidence: high, 2 independent databases.

**Family:** Armadillidiidae (WoRMS + GBIF). Confidence: high, 2.
- Conflict: Exuvium lists the family as "Armadillidae" (https://exuvium.net/cristarmadillidium-muricatum/). That is a seller error or older placement; the databases agree on Armadillidiidae. Use the databases.

**Genus:** Cristarmadillidium. **Basionym / old name:** Armadillidium muricatum (Exuvium, same URL; WoRMS shows an original-name record, originalNameUsageID 294178). Confidence: medium-high, 2.

**Common / trade names:** Pineapple Spiky, Crystal Pineapple, European Spiny Pill Woodlouse / European Spiny Isopod (Exuvium https://exuvium.net/cristarmadillidium-muricatum/); "Crystal Pineapple" (Invertopia https://invertopia.co.uk/products/crystal-pineapple-isopods-cristarmadillidium-muricatum); "Pineapple Spiky Isopod" (Bio Dude https://www.thebiodude.com/blogs/isopod-and-springtail-clean-up-crew-guides-culturing-care-and-breeding/pineapple-spiky-isopod-care-sheet). Confidence: medium, 3 sellers (names only; no scientific source).

**Colour morphs in trade:** None found. "Crystal Pineapple" and "Pineapple" are trade names for the species, not morphs. Invertopia describes colour as pale yellow to soft orange. Value: no morphs documented. Confidence: low (absence of evidence, 3 sellers).

**Native range:** Spain. Exuvium says Alicante, Valencia and Palma de Mallorca (https://exuvium.net/cristarmadillidium-muricatum/); Invertopia says Spain, "dry Mediterranean regions" (link above). I did not open a scientific distribution source (two possible leads appeared in search but were not read: https://balearica.uib.cat/es/taxon/9511 and a Graellsia paper at graellsia.revistas.csic.es). Confidence: medium, 2 sellers, consistent.
- Conflict: Bio Dude calls it a "tropical isopod ... associated with humid forest environments". This contradicts the two Spanish-origin, dry-Mediterranean descriptions and the Spanish locality list. Treat the Bio Dude habitat text as unreliable.

**Adult size:** 6-7 mm (Exuvium) and 6-8 mm (Invertopia) = about 0.24-0.31 in. Bio Dude gives no size. Confidence: medium, 2 sellers, consistent. Recommend 6-8 mm (0.24-0.31 in), hobby-consensus only.

**Care level:** Intermediate. Reasons: needs good ventilation and a moisture gradient rather than damp-throughout; slow-ish grower; not an explosive breeder. Exuvium "easy to intermediate"; Invertopia "medium"; Bio Dude "intermediate to advanced". Confidence: medium, 3 sources agree on "not beginner".

**Temperature:** Sources disagree.
- Exuvium: 17-30 C (63-86 F), notes summers in Alicante exceed 30 C.
- Invertopia: 18-24 C (64-75 F).
- Bio Dude: ideal 72-78 F, tolerable 65-82 F.
- The overlap of all three is roughly 65-75 F. Recommend storing 65-78 F as the "keeper-reported comfort range" with a note that seller ranges differ; or leave the upper bound null. Confidence: low-medium, 3 sources, partially conflicting. Hobby-consensus only.

**Humidity:** Exuvium 50-70%; Invertopia 50-70% (two agree). Bio Dude says ideal 70-85% (outlier, tied to its "tropical" description). Recommend 50-70%. Confidence: medium, 2 agree vs 1 outlier. Exuvium's FAQ also says they do not need higher humidity than Armadillidium.

**Substrate and moisture setup:** Gradient, not damp throughout. Exuvium: keep about half moist with moss, rotting white wood and decaying leaves. Invertopia: moisture gradient is "essential", sphagnum in one section only, rest relatively dry and well aired, ventilation "never an afterthought", moderate substrate depth with light burrowing, "dry woodland floor, not tropical rainforest". Bio Dude: low ventilation with steady airflow, damp retreat zones, layered cork bark. Confidence: medium, 3 sources agree on a gradient; they differ on how wet. Ventilation: Invertopia says high, Bio Dude "low ventilation with steady airflow" - mild conflict.

**Diet:** Detritivore. Leaf litter and decaying wood as staple; small amounts of vegetables; protein (fish flakes, dried shrimp) occasionally (Exuvium says twice a week; Bio Dude says it is a lighter feeder than most and warns against overfeeding because of mould; Invertopia says "occasional" protein). Calcium source always available (cuttlebone, limestone, eggshell) - all three sources say this. Confidence: medium-high, 3 sources.

**Behaviour:**
- Conglobation: yes, rolls into a ball when disturbed (Invertopia; Bio Dude). Confidence: medium, 2 sellers.
- Activity: nocturnal / early morning (Exuvium; Invertopia says mostly nocturnal or low light). Shy, hides readily (Invertopia).
- Speed, climbing, burrowing: Invertopia says "light burrowing"; speed and climbing not stated. NULL for speed and climbing.

**Growth / reproduction:** Exuvium: growth "normal to slow", reproduction "fair", lifespan 2-3 years for captive-bred. Invertopia: breeds at a steady, manageable pace, "not explosive". Bio Dude: slower to establish than common clean-up species. Confidence: medium, 3 sellers agree on moderate-to-slow.

**Special notes:** Do not keep waterlogged or stagnant. Easy to over-feed and get mould (Bio Dude). Best as a display species rather than a clean-up crew (Bio Dude). Legal/import: NULL (nothing found).

## 2. Porcellio scaber

**Accepted name and authority:** Porcellio scaber Latreille, 1804. Status accepted.
- WoRMS: https://www.marinespecies.org/aphia.php?p=taxdetails&id=262440
- GBIF: usageKey 6284337, ACCEPTED (https://api.gbif.org/v1/species/match?name=Porcellio%20scaber&verbose=true)
- Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcellio_scaber
- Confidence: high, 3.

**Family / genus:** Porcellionidae / Porcellio (WoRMS, GBIF, Wikipedia). Confidence: high, 3.

**Common / trade names:** Common rough woodlouse, rough woodlouse (Wikipedia); Common Rough Woodlouse (Jack of all Inverts https://jackofallinverts.github.io/caresheets/pscaber.html). Hobby colour-morph names seen on vendor product titles: Dalmatian, Moonstone, Calico, Ghost, Piebald, White Out, Koi, Lava, Yellow Snow (Exuvium, Josh's Frogs, Terrarium Tribe shop - found via search; titles only, pages not opened). Confidence: medium, vendor titles from several shops.

**Colour morphs in trade:** Yes, many; these are cultivated morphs of the one species, not separate species (Wikipedia: "rare colour morphs are commonly cultivated in captivity"; Jack of all Inverts: "wide variety of colour morphs ... new combinations are discovered often"). Wikipedia also notes bright blue individuals are caused by an iridovirus infection, not a natural morph - worth a warning note in the guide. Confidence: high, 3 sources (Wikipedia, Jack of all Inverts, vendor listings).

**Native range:** Native to Central and Western Europe; now cosmopolitan through human activity (North America, South Africa, Australia, sub-Antarctic Marion Island). Source: Wikipedia, citing Fauna Europaea and Slabber and Chown 2002 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcellio_scaber). Jack of all Inverts also says cosmopolitan. Confidence: high, 2 (secondary source citing primary).

**Adult size:** Up to 20 mm (0.79 in). Wikipedia says "up to 20 millimetres (0.79 in)"; Jack of all Inverts "up to 20mm (0.79in)"; Leaf Litter Lab 15-20 mm. Confidence: high, 3 agree. Record as "up to about 20 mm (0.79 in)"; a typical range of 15-20 mm rests on the weak Leaf Litter Lab source only.

**Care level:** Beginner. Reasons: hardy, tolerates drier conditions, breeds readily. Jack of all Inverts: "great for beginners due to their hardiness"; Leaf Litter Lab: "perfect for beginners"; Wikipedia: less dependent on high humidity than Oniscus asellus. Confidence: high, 3.

**Temperature:** Jack of all Inverts 65-80 F (18.3-26.6 C). Leaf Litter Lab 64-75 F (18-24 C). Search excerpts only (not opened): Bio Dude tolerable 65-90 F; Josh's Frogs 60-85 F; Exuvium about 17-26 C. Recommend 65-80 F. Confidence: medium, 2 opened sources overlap, 3 more in excerpts agree broadly.

**Humidity:** Sources disagree: Jack of all Inverts 50-60% ("drought tolerant"); Leaf Litter Lab 60-80%; search excerpts only: Bio Dude 50-75%, Furry Critter 70-80%, Exuvium 50-60%. No consensus number. Recommend 50-70% with "keep a damp corner" and tolerance of drier air. Confidence: low-medium, wide spread of seller numbers. Hobby-consensus only.

**Substrate and moisture setup:** Gradient. Jack of all Inverts: organic peat, decayed wood, flake soil, sphagnum, compost, leaves, plus calcium; moist hides and substrate; ventilation present; cork or oak bark hides. Leaf Litter Lab: slightly moist but not wet, 5-10 cm depth, cork bark, leaf litter, sphagnum (weak source). Search excerpt: Exuvium keep about one third moist. Confidence: medium, 2 opened sources agree.

**Diet:** Detritivore. Wikipedia: mainly feeds on decaying leaf litter and the bacteria and fungi on it. Jack of all Inverts: "very protein hungry", may prefer animal products; offers leaves and rotting wood as staple plus fruit, vegetables, pet kibble, fish food, dried fish or shrimp; calcium (eggshell or calcium carbonate). Confidence: high on detritivore, medium on protein emphasis (1 hobby source stressing it; Leaf Litter Lab lists fish flakes and calcium).

**Behaviour:**
- Conglobation: NO. It is a "clinger" and freezes (tonic immobility) when threatened, unlike Armadillidium "rollers" (Wikipedia, citing Tuf et al. 2015). Confidence: high (secondary source citing a published study).
- Speed: active; Leaf Litter Lab calls it "very active". Wikipedia mentions escape behaviour studies. Treat as a fairly fast, active isopod; no quantified speed. Confidence: low.
- Climbing / burrowing: not stated in opened sources. NULL.

**Growth / reproduction:** Females carry about 25-90 eggs in a brood pouch (Jack of all Inverts; Wikipedia). Time in the pouch: Wikipedia says about 40-50 days; Leaf Litter Lab says 3-4 weeks - conflict, use Wikipedia (cites a Zurich city pest-advice sheet). Mature in about 3 months (Wikipedia; Jack of all Inverts "as little as 3 months"). Lifespan about 2-3 years in captivity (Jack of all Inverts; Leaf Litter Lab), about 2 years (Wikipedia). Jack of all Inverts reports about 40 animals growing to over 600 in 2 years when well fed. Confidence: medium-high, 2-3 sources.

**Special notes:** Fast breeder, can outgrow a container. Eats its own moults (Jack of all Inverts). Does not conglobate, so it cannot defend itself by rolling; dry air still dehydrates it because it has no waxy cuticle (Wikipedia). Legal/import: NULL.

## 3. Porcellio dilatatus ("Giant Canyon")

**Accepted name and authority:** Porcellio dilatatus Brandt in Brandt and Ratzeburg, 1831 (WoRMS and GBIF).
- WoRMS: https://www.marinespecies.org/aphia.php?p=taxdetails&id=262272
- GBIF: usageKey 6284372, "Brandt, 1831", ACCEPTED
- Conflict: Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcellio_dilatatus) gives "Brandt, 1833". The two databases agree on 1831; use 1831. Confidence: high, 2 databases.

**Family / genus:** Porcellionidae / Porcellio. Confidence: high, 3 (WoRMS, GBIF, Wikipedia).

**Common / trade names:** Giant Canyon, Giant Canyon Isopod, Giant Canyon Woodlouse (Exuvium https://exuvium.net/porcellio-dilatatus/); Giant Canyon Isopod, Canyon Isopod (Bio Dude https://www.thebiodude.com/blogs/isopod-and-springtail-clean-up-crew-guides-culturing-care-and-breeding/giant-canyon-isopod-care-sheet); Dutch "Brede Pissebed" (Exuvium). "Giant Canyon" is a common name of the species itself, not a locale or morph. Confidence: high, 3.
- Note: Exuvium lists old varieties "var. flavus" and "var. rufobrunneus"; Wikipedia, citing WoRMS, lists five subspecies including P. d. flavus Collinge 1917 and P. d. rufobrunneus Collinge 1918. Colour morphs in the trade: none found. Confidence: low (absence of evidence).

**Native range:** Sources disagree.
- Wikipedia: widespread in Europe; introduced to North America from Western Europe (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcellio_dilatatus).
- Exuvium: Western Europe.
- Bio Dude: Europe and Mediterranean regions (grassland, scrub, rocky, dry woodland).
- Search excerpts (not opened) also claimed western US/northern Mexico "canyon walls"; I could not verify this and it looks like it comes from the trade name. Do not use it.
- Recommend: "Europe (Western Europe and the Mediterranean); introduced elsewhere, including North America." Confidence: medium, 3 sources; wording kept general because they differ.

**Adult size:** Exuvium 20 mm; Wikipedia 15 mm; search excerpt (not opened) gives 18-22 mm from one seller. 15-20 mm = about 0.59-0.79 in. Recommend about 15-20 mm (0.6-0.8 in). Confidence: medium, 2 opened sources, ranges overlap but do not match.

**Care level:** Beginner to intermediate; sources split. Exuvium: "easy", "suitable for beginners". Bio Dude: "Intermediate". Terrarium Tribe (https://terrariumtribe.com/porcellio-hoffmannseggi/, which recommends it for dry terrariums). Recommend "beginner-intermediate", reason: hardy and drought tolerant, but large with a big appetite and slower breeding than many Porcellio (Bio Dude). Confidence: medium, 2 opened sources, disagree by one level.

**Temperature:** Exuvium 15-26 C (59-79 F). Bio Dude ideal 72-82 F, tolerable 65-90 F. Search excerpts (not opened): 70-80 F and 75-85 F from other sellers. Recommend 65-82 F; the top end is the least certain. Confidence: medium-low, 2 opened sources, partly overlapping.

**Humidity:** Exuvium 50-70% ("drought tolerant"); Bio Dude ideal 40-65%. Recommend 40-70%. Confidence: medium, 2 sources overlap.

**Substrate and moisture setup:** Gradient with a damp retreat and good ventilation. Exuvium: keep about one third moist; they dig, so substrate at least 5-10 cm deep, and they also eat it. Bio Dude: deep substrate, leaf litter, cork bark, moderate-to-high ventilation, damp retreat zones. Confidence: medium-high, 2 sources agree.

**Diet:** Detritivore / saprophage (Wikipedia). Exuvium: not picky, "voracious appetite", protein twice a week plus calcium. Bio Dude: regular protein and constant calcium because of strong appetite and large size. Confidence: high, 3 sources.

**Behaviour:** Conglobation: NO (genus Porcellio are clingers; not stated for this species in the opened sources, so NULL for species-specific confirmation; Wikipedia for P. scaber documents the genus pattern only). Nocturnal / early morning (Exuvium). Burrowing: yes, likes to dig (Exuvium; Bio Dude "natural burrowing zones"). Speed and climbing: not stated. NULL.

**Growth / reproduction:** Exuvium: reproduction "fair", growth "normal". Bio Dude: slower reproduction rate than some. Confidence: medium, 2.

**Special notes:** Wikipedia also lists it as a minor pest in greenhouses and flower pots. Terrarium Tribe suggests it as a better bioactive clean-up species for dry terrariums than the titan isopod. Legal/import: NULL.

## 4. Armadillo officinalis

**Accepted name and authority:** Armadillo officinalis Duméril, 1816. WoRMS gives "Dumeril, 1816", status accepted, AphiaID 259095 (https://www.marinespecies.org/aphia.php?p=taxdetails&id=259095). GBIF: "Armadillo officinalis Duméril, 1816", ACCEPTED, usageKey 2202347. Confidence: high, 2 databases.

**Family / genus:** Armadillidae / Armadillo (WoRMS, GBIF; Exuvium agrees on Armadillidae). Confidence: high, 3.
- Warning: Exuvium's listing gives "Basionym: Lyprobius lentus" and synonyms "Cubaris invenustus, Pentheus punctatus" (https://exuvium.net/armadillo-officinalis-israel-isopods-for-sale/ and the Pink listing). I could not confirm these in WoRMS; they may be wrong or may refer to the Israeli population. Do not copy them into the guide. Confidence on those names: low.

**Common / trade names:** Spanish Pill Bug (Pangea, via search), Hissing Isopod (Richard's Inverts https://richardsinverts-store.com/products/armadillo-officinalis-orange-crush.json), Sicily locale (DubiaRoaches, via search). Confidence: medium, vendor names only.

**Colour morphs / locales in trade:** Both exist and should not be mixed up.
- Cultivated colour morphs (same species): Orange Crush (Richard's Inverts: "selectively bred for their mutation that changes their colour from the wild-type grey to orange"), Pink and Pink Dalmatian (Exuvium https://exuvium.net/armadillo-officinalis-pink-isopods-for-sale/, which says "cultured variety"), White Piña (Exuvium listing title).
- Locales: Israel (Exuvium: a different, silvery pattern with orange spots), Tamarix, Sicily (Exuvium / DubiaRoaches listings).
- Exuvium notes a subspecies, A. officinalis syriacus. Confidence: medium, 3 sellers.

**Native range:** Mediterranean. I did not open a scientific distribution source; a search excerpt of Plazi / regional checklists described it as occurring across the Mediterranean basin, Morocco and Portugal (excerpt only, not verified). Vendor locales include Israel, Sicily and Spain. Recommend "Mediterranean region" with no finer claim. Confidence: low-medium, 1 excerpt plus vendor locales.

**Adult size:** Sources disagree strongly. Exuvium: 20 mm for the Pink and Israel forms (about 0.79 in). A taxonomic redescription (Charfi-Cheikhrouha et al., seen only in a search excerpt) gives males about 12.5 mm and females about 10.5 mm. These cannot both be right as a single figure. Recommend NULL for a single number, or record "about 10-20 mm (0.4-0.8 in), sources disagree". Confidence: low.

**Care level:** Beginner to intermediate. Exuvium: "easy" (both opened listings); Richard's Inverts tags the Orange Crush "beginner-friendly" and "easy". Confidence: medium, 2 sellers; no independent view.

**Temperature:** Exuvium 15-28 C (59-82 F); Richard's 18-26 C (64-79 F). Recommend 64-80 F. Confidence: medium, 2 sellers overlap.

**Humidity:** Exuvium 55-70% ("adaptable"); Richard's "medium". Recommend 55-70%. Confidence: medium, 2.

**Substrate and moisture setup:** Exuvium: keep about two thirds moist with moss, bark and leaf litter (wetter than the Porcellio and Cristarmadillidium listings). Richard's: medium humidity, medium airflow. Search excerpt: moderately humid with plenty of hiding spots. Confidence: medium, 2 opened.

**Diet:** Detritivore. Decaying wood and leaf litter, vegetables, fish food, calcium (cuttlebone, limestone, oyster shell). Exuvium: a "slightly higher protein" diet, at least twice a week (both listings). Richard's: calcium and fish food as supplements. Confidence: medium, 2.

**Behaviour:** Conglobation: yes, "to a perfect ball" (Exuvium, both listings). Stridulation/hissing: Richard's says it makes a hissing sound when disturbed by rubbing its legs inside the exoskeleton - this explains the "Hissing Isopod" trade name; I could not verify the mechanism scientifically (a search excerpt mentioned a scientific study of its stridulation apparatus, not opened). Nocturnal / early morning (Exuvium). Speed, burrowing, climbing: NULL. Confidence: medium, 2 sellers.

**Growth / reproduction:** Exuvium: reproduction "prolific", growth "slow" (both listings). Confidence: medium, 1 seller across 2 listings (not independent).

**Special notes:** Hobby colonies include several named forms; keep locales and cultivated morphs separate to avoid mislabelling. Legal/import: NULL.

## 5. Merulanella sp. "Ember Bee"

**Is it an undescribed species sold under a trade name? Yes, with caveats.**
- It carries no formal species name anywhere I looked. Every vendor writes "sp." with "Ember Bee" in quotes as a trade name. Exuvium: "Scientific name: Ardentiella sp., Trade name: Ember Bee, Synonyms: Merulanella sp." (https://exuvium.net/merulanella-sp-ember-bee-isopods-for-sale/). Richard's Inverts: "Ardentiella (Merulanella) sp. 'Ember Bee'" (https://richardsinverts-store.com/products/ardentiella-sp-ember-bee.json). Confidence: high that it is an undescribed taxon, 2 vendors plus the genus paper's statement that further species await description.
- Genus: sellers filed it as Merulanella until 2025, and have since moved it to Ardentiella. The change follows a 2025 paper: Kästle and Regalado Fernández, "Facing the taxonomic impediment - a reassessment of Merulanella Verhoeff, 1926 (Oniscidea, Armadillidae) through historical specimens", Natural History Collections and Museomics 2: 1-38, https://doi.org/10.3897/nhcm.2.144386 (open access, CC BY). The paper erects the genus Ardentiella Kästle and Regalado Fernández, 2025 (WoRMS AphiaID 1804749, accepted, family Armadillidae: https://www.marinespecies.org/aphia.php?p=taxdetails&id=1804749). Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardentiella) says the described species are A. bicolorata and A. caerulea from Myanmar and Thailand, with further undescribed species from China, Laos and Vietnam. The old genus Merulanella Verhoeff, 1926 is still listed as accepted (WoRMS AphiaID 248944; GBIF genusKey 2202952, Armadillidae).
- What I could not verify: that "Ember Bee" specifically belongs in Ardentiella. The only support is the sellers' own re-filing plus Wikipedia's statement about undescribed Vietnamese species. I did not read the paper's full text (only its abstract page) and nothing I read names "Ember Bee". Confidence on genus Ardentiella: low-medium, 2 vendors + 1 secondary summary, no direct taxonomic confirmation.
- Recommendation for the catalog: scientific_name = "Ardentiella sp. 'Ember Bee'" (or keep "Merulanella sp. 'Ember Bee'" if the existing catalog uses trade-name spelling), family Armadillidae, flag as undescribed / trade name, accepted authority NULL. Add "Merulanella sp." as a synonym so searches for the older name still match.

**Family:** Armadillidae (WoRMS for both genera; Exuvium). Confidence: high, 3.

**Common / trade names:** "Ember Bee" (all sources). Other trade names in the same group: Red Diablo, Tricolor, Black Hole, Yellow Phoenix, Pink Lambo, Blister (Exuvium / Morphmarket links) - these are separate undescribed forms, not morphs of Ember Bee. Colour morphs of Ember Bee itself: none found. Confidence: medium, 2 vendors.

**Native range:** Vietnam (Exuvium; the Exuvium page lists "Origin: Vietnam"). Search excerpt describes it as colorful isopod from Vietnam (single source for locality). Confidence: low, 1 vendor.

**Adult size:** Up to 23 mm (Exuvium) = about 0.9 in. Richard's says "humongous" and "1.5x larger" than other Ardentiella. Confidence: low-medium, 1 numeric source.

**Care level:** Intermediate. Reasons: wild-caught animals are hard to keep; captive-bred lines are easier, but it is a fussy, rare, expensive species needing high humidity and high airflow together. Exuvium: "moderate to easy for CB isopods", wild-caught "quite challenging", "not a beginner isopod". Richard's: "high for wild caught, medium for captive bred", "super rare". Confidence: medium, 2 vendors agree.

**Temperature:** Richard's 18-24 C (64-75 F); Exuvium 19-26 C (66-79 F), "prefer it a bit cooler". Recommend 66-75 F. Confidence: medium, 2 agree. Hobby-consensus only.

**Humidity:** Richard's "high" (no number); Exuvium 60-75%, keep wetter like Cubaris and mist periodically. Recommend 60-75%. Confidence: medium-low, 1 numeric source.

**Substrate and moisture setup:** Layered setup of decaying leaves, forest moss, twigs with lichen, topped with cork or tree bark; kept damp but with high ventilation/airflow (Richard's: ventilation "a must"). Needs tall climbing space and an escape-proof lid: young animals can climb vertical plastic (Exuvium). Confidence: medium, 2.

**Diet:** Fruit, vegetables, greens, forest moss, lichen, decaying leaves, rotting white wood, some protein (gammarus shrimp), calcium (cuttlebone, limestone). They do not eat bee pollen, and do not eat live plants (Exuvium FAQ). Richard's: forest materials plus calcium and fish food. Confidence: medium, 2.

**Behaviour:** Conglobation: NULL (not stated in sources read; the genus is in Armadillidae, which often conglobate, but I will not assume it). Active by day and night, mostly at night; "quite active" and bold (Exuvium; Richard's). Climbing: yes, arboreal; prefers a taller enclosure (Exuvium FAQ; Richard's). Burrowing and speed: NULL. Confidence: medium, 2.

**Growth / reproduction:** Exuvium: growth normal to slow; reproduction fair to prolific for captive-bred, challenging for wild-caught. Richard's: stabilised captive-bred lines breed readily. Confidence: medium-low, 2 vendors.

**Special notes:**
- The 2025 paper states that some species in this group are considered endangered because of the pet trade, although they are not on the IUCN Red List (abstract: https://doi.org/10.3897/nhcm.2.144386). This is the main reason to prefer captive-bred animals. Confidence: high (published abstract).
- Wild-caught import is the usual source of early deaths (Richard's, Exuvium).
- Legal/import status in the US: NULL (not found).
- Very scarce and expensive: Richard's lists a single animal at GBP 15 (price at fetch time; do not publish prices).

## 6. Porcellio hoffmannseggii (Titan isopod)

**Accepted name and authority:** Porcellio hoffmannseggii Brandt, 1833. Status accepted.
- WoRMS: https://www.marinespecies.org/aphia.php?p=taxdetails&id=262325
- GBIF: usageKey 6284350, ACCEPTED
- Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcellio_hoffmannseggii
- Confidence: high, 3.
- Spelling: the double-i form is correct. The single-i spelling "hoffmannseggi" is common in the trade and on the Terrarium Tribe page title. Store the correct spelling and keep the misspelling as a searchable alias.
- Do not confuse with Platyarthrus hoffmannseggii, the tiny ant woodlouse (Wikipedia). Care sheets for that species do not apply.

**Family / genus:** Porcellionidae / Porcellio. Confidence: high, 3.

**Common / trade names:** Titan isopod / "Titan" (Wikipedia; Terrarium Tribe https://terrariumtribe.com/porcellio-hoffmannseggi/). Confidence: high, 2.

**Colour morphs in trade:** Yes, same species. Terrarium Tribe lists "Black" (technically a locale), "Brown" / "Chocolate", "White Out" (a rare albino form, recently isolated); the Orange Hoffmannseggi and Ultra White Antenna forms also appear in Exuvium product titles (titles only). Typical colour is muted grey with a white skirt. Confidence: medium, 2 (Terrarium Tribe + vendor titles).

**Native range:** Southern Iberian Peninsula (southern Spain, Portugal), Morocco and the Balearic Islands (Wikipedia, citing Schmalfuss 1987, "Revision der Gattung Porcellio ... P. hoffmannseggi und P. magnificus"; https://digital.csic.es/handle/10261/171968). Terrarium Tribe: southern Spain and surrounding areas, citing the same paper. I did not open the paper itself. Confidence: medium-high, 2 secondary sources both citing one primary.

**Adult size:** Wikipedia: over 1.5 in (3.8 cm) long; Terrarium Tribe: "many reaching up to 4 cm" (about 1.6 in). Recommend up to about 38-40 mm (1.5-1.6 in). Males have longer uropods than females (Wikipedia, Terrarium Tribe). Confidence: medium, 2 agree.

**Care level:** Intermediate. Reasons: needs space and dry-leaning conditions with a steep moisture gradient, territorial (adults can injure each other), and survival and breeding are reported to be inconsistent. Terrarium Tribe: "far less forgiving than the beginner-friendly species" and "isn't too challenging overall" (mixed). Wikipedia: territorial, "makes captive culturing more difficult". Search excerpts (not opened) described some guides as relatively low maintenance. Confidence: medium-low, 2 opened sources, mixed wording.

**Temperature:** Terrarium Tribe: room temperature, above about 68 F (20 C). A search excerpt gave 24-27 C (75-81 F) for one guide - single source, not opened. Recommend NULL for an upper bound, lower comfort bound about 68 F, or record "room temperature, roughly 68-80 F" as low confidence. Confidence: low, 1 opened source for the lower bound.

**Humidity:** Unsettled.
- Terrarium Tribe: prefers drier, low-humidity conditions with plenty of ventilation; keep humidity as low as reasonably possible, but still needs a damp retreat (steep moisture gradient with sphagnum).
- A reader comment on the same page (an experienced keeper) reports they seek out the wettest corner and do fine at 50-70% in a damp zone, and that many breeders keep them in ordinary humid conditions with regular misting.
- A search excerpt gave 75-85% from another guide (not opened; looks out of line with the above).
- Recommend NULL for a numeric humidity range, and describe it as "dry-leaning with a damp corner; keepers disagree". Confidence: low, sources conflict.

**Substrate and moisture setup:** Gradient. Roomy ventilated container (e.g. shoebox-size with large air holes), plenty of separate hides (branches, egg cartons), damp sphagnum corner, rest drier. Divide colonies aggressively into small groups. Source: Terrarium Tribe (single detailed source; Wikipedia supports "prefers drier environments"). Confidence: medium, 2 sources.

**Diet:** Detritivore. Reported to prefer decaying wood over leaf litter; also usual isopod foods, regular calcium, some protein. Low feeding response: "pretty passive" about food, so poor as a clean-up crew (Terrarium Tribe). Confidence: medium-low, 1 detailed source.

**Behaviour:**
- Conglobation: NO (Wikipedia).
- Territorial toward each other, can cause serious injuries; females guard young for the first months (Terrarium Tribe; Wikipedia says they guard young after birth). Confidence: medium, 2.
- Speed: "slow and easy to spot" and easy to handle (Terrarium Tribe). Confidence: low, 1 source.
- Burrowing, climbing: NULL.

**Growth / reproduction:** Terrarium Tribe: breeding "tends to be quick and easy with average-sized broods" in a healthy, stable culture, though some Reddit keepers report poor survival and reproduction, possibly because of aggression. Growth rate: NULL. Confidence: low, 1 source with a self-reported mixed picture.

**Special notes:**
- May be eaten by larger reptiles; too slow-feeding to be a good bioactive clean-up species; consider P. dilatatus instead (Terrarium Tribe).
- Aggression between adults is the leading suspected cause of failing cultures: give space and hides.
